## Reference: 
 * Bollenbacher, J., Loynes, N. and Bryden, J. (2022) Does United Kingdom parliamentary attention follow social media posts? EPJ Data Science 11(1), p. 51
## Discussion Points: 
* This paper investigates the relationship between social media posts and parliamentary attention in the UK, which is relevant to our analysis of parliamentary questions in Ireland.
* The findings of this study suggest there is a correlation between social media activity and the topics discussed in parliamentary questions.
* The authors conclude that they have found clear evidence of directional word flows between domains (social media and parliamentary questions), which supports the idea that social media can influence parliamentary discourse.
# Limitations of paper:
* The study is limited to the UK context, and the findings may not be directly applicable to the Irish context due to differences in political systems and social media usage patterns.
* The analysis focuses on a specific time period, which may not capture long-term trends or changes in the relationship between social media and parliamentary attention.
* The study relies on the assumption that social media posts are a significant driver of parliamentary attention, which may not always be the case, as other factors such as media coverage and public opinion can also influence parliamentary discourse.
